Something I aim for is a **complete view**. Seeing things from multiple sides and angles.

Sometimes that means steel-manning someone else's argument. Sometimes that means presenting a side that's been neglected: I find this one to frequently be the case with AI, in particular hype-based reporting. One of the reasons that I went to an AI conference and [took a bunch of notes](/2026/06/07/web-directions-ai-engineers/) was to get a more complete view of how jobbing engineers are using AI. What's working and what's not. To get a more complete view of AI in engineering.

<!-- I value clarity. But/and that doesn't usually mean certainty. I value truth. But/and that's usually about degrees and perspectives. -->

Realistically, it's impossible to have a (completely) complete view: to consider every single angle. But we can work towards a *more* complete view. One that takes in more perspectives. There are a few ways that I try and come to a more complete view: wider, deeper, more focused. The main thing is to ask a lot of questions! I find it helpful to have a few "grips" to get a handle on the process.

## Wider

**Look at the wider context**, make more sense of it.

What else is happening? Why this, and why now? What parts of the story are being left out?

## Deeper

**Dig deeper into the details**, ask for specifics.

When someone says "massive improvements in productivity", for example, ask for specifics. What was the improvement? What was measured? Over what time period?

## More focused

**Bring it into clearer focus**, actively work through it rather passively consume it.

Gain a better understanding by working the material.
