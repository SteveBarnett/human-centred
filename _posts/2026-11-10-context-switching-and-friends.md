Splitting time on a project into small chunks, especially with gaps of a day or more between, adds a hefty context switching tax. Related: a 100% full schedule has bad effects on us, our work, and our colleagues.

## Context switching

Some of the things that can take a hit:

- we compromise the quality of our work
    - we lose some of our attention to detail
    - we leave things unfinished
- we're less productive: the same amount of work takes longer overall
- we create technical debt (and design debt and ...)
- we're more mentally tired from the extra effort of switching and re-switching

In particular, coming back to an accessibility audit after a context switch takes a long time. We deliver our best work on audits when we can keep a lot of it in our head at once.

### Limiting WIP (Work In Progress)

More WIP means:

- more things that could go wrong
- more things taking up head space, apart from just the one thing we're working on right now
- a feeling of being productive because we're touching lots of tasks (but that's not the same as actually being more productive)
- less time and space to think
- more context switching!

## Not booking to 100%

When there's some slack in the schedule, when we're not booked to 100%:

- we have capacity to deal with unexpected things and interruptions
- we give our brains time to rest, process, recharge
- we have room to correct any errors
- we have time to improve through reflection and adaptation
- we can be creative because we have time to be
- we are able to help our colleagues
- we can learn new skills

## What to do

- limit context switching, so that we can keep delivering high quality work and don't get burnt out
- limit WIP, so that we finish things and are more productive
- don't book to 100% capacity, so that we don't feel rushed and can handle interruptions and unexpected events more gracefully
